Analysis of Shanghai Residents’ Recognition of Foreign Tourists

Research Analyst · Quantitative Survey Study

  • Designed and executed a two-phase quantitative survey collecting 99 responses; performed rigorous data cleaning and descriptive statistical analysis (means, medians, modes, standard deviations) across demographic variables and seven recognition dimensions. Established baseline attitudes and identified cultural-diversity recognition (mean = 4.10) as the strongest positive dimension.
  • Conducted one-way ANOVA to test the effects of gender, age, length of residence and occupation on attitude variables; identified age as the primary significant factor (multiple p < 0.05, including p < 0.001 for international-image perception). Followed with Pearson chi-square cross-tabulation to pinpoint the highest recognition concentration among the 30–50 age group and retail/commerce occupations.
  • Applied Spearman rank-order correlation to quantify relationships between overall positive attitude and six key predictors, revealing strong positive associations with cultural-diversity enrichment (r = 0.817, p < 0.001), international-image enhancement (r = 0.607, p < 0.001) and economic contribution (r = 0.567, p < 0.001). Environmental-pressure perception showed no significant link (r ≈ 0). Translated results into targeted policy recommendations.
